Managed AI Workforce & Data Operations

Managed AI data teams. Specialist and generalist workstreams. Accountable delivery.

Scaling AI data and LLM projects takes more than allocating tasks. Buyers need the right specialists and generalist reviewers, consistent instructions, quality leads, transparent reporting and accountable operations. Frostrek AI provides managed teams for AI training data, human evaluation, model-quality programs and broader data operations.

Our managed delivery model can cover hiring, screening, training, calibration, task execution, quality review, escalation, reporting and day-to-day delivery management. Team size and ramp-up are scoped to the project; we do not advertise an unverified fixed bench or guaranteed headcount.

Core Delivery Pillars

Managed delivery services

Structured human operations ensuring task consistency, quality compliance, and responsive escalation.

Workforce planning and reviewer qualification

Frostrek can recruit, qualify, onboard and train annotators, generalist evaluators, language reviewers, coding/STEM specialists, domain experts and quality leads to match task complexity and volume. Actual qualifications, availability and onboarding schedules are established for each engagement.

Delivery component 01

Team leadership and delivery coordination

Frostrek manages the delivery workflow: staffing, team leadership, task allocation, daily coordination, client reporting, issue escalation and project management. Named leaders and responsibilities are documented during onboarding.

Delivery component 02

Reviewer calibration and process documentation

AI tasks may involve subjective judgments or incomplete examples. We can structure reviewer onboarding around client-approved rubrics, examples and documented decisions. When guidelines change, teams need version control, explanation of revised rules and a practical process for handling items already in progress.

Delivery component 03

Multi-level quality checks and exception handling

Our managed QA process can include quality leads, peer review, sampling, independent second-pass checks, issue logs, adjudication, corrective coaching and controlled rework. The client and Frostrek agree on the sampling method and acceptance criteria for each project; no blanket accuracy figure is promised.

Delivery component 04

Progress reporting and client communication

A useful operations report should distinguish assigned, completed, accepted, rejected and reworked items, along with quality trends and unresolved blockers. Frostrek can define reporting and escalation routines with the client based on their tools, decision cycles and data-delivery requirements.

Delivery component 05

Scoped ramp-up and capacity adjustments

When program volume changes, Frostrek can plan recruitment, training, specialist onboarding and QA coverage to scale the team. Headcount and start dates are confirmed against live availability and project scope; an applicant or recruitment pipeline is not described as active deployed staff.

Delivery Framework

How a managed AI workforce engagement works

A clear five-step governance process from initial task definition to iterative operational refinement.

01Stage 1 • Scope
Stage 1 • Scope

Define tasks, tools and decision owners

We review sample inputs, desired outputs, accepted/rejected examples, workflow tooling, permitted access and escalation contacts. The scoping discussion separates what Frostrek owns from what the client retains, including rubric decisions, data access approval and acceptance sign-off.

02Stage 2 • Onboard
Stage 2 • Onboard

Establish the team and calibrate reviewers

Match roles to domain requirements, onboard against representative tasks and check consistency before committing to sustained volume. Document unclear instructions and get approval for interpretation changes rather than relying on informal judgments.

03Stage 3 • Execution
Stage 3 • Execution

Execute a controlled workstream

Begin with an agreed initial batch or clearly defined recurring delivery cadence. Route new edge cases, technical blockers and policy questions to accountable owners. Where appropriate, separate production activity from quality decisions.

04Stage 4 • Quality Controls
Stage 4 • Quality Controls

Apply QA and course correction

Measure performance using the client-approved sampling plan and acceptance criteria. Record error categories, investigate repeat issues, rework affected items and retrain reviewers or clarify guidelines as the program requires.

05Stage 5 • Governance
Stage 5 • Governance

Report outcomes and improve the operating model

Share agreed operational summaries, workload visibility, QA observations and unresolved exceptions. Adjust staffing mix, calibration material or workflow design based on evidence and approved change requests.

Commercial Flexibility

Engagement models that can be scoped

Flexible operational structures matched to ongoing or project-based delivery needs.

Ongoing workstreams

Dedicated managed team

A defined group of reviewers, quality leads and delivery coordination roles assigned to an ongoing workstream, with reporting responsibilities and continuity arrangements documented in the agreement.

Fixed-scope programs

Project-based data operations

A bounded annotation, evaluation or validation program with agreed deliverables, tool access, QA criteria and acceptance procedure. Suitable when volumes and deadlines can be scoped ahead of execution.

Independent second-pass QA

Quality-review support

A focused QA or adjudication workstream alongside a client-operated production team, subject to access permissions, independence requirements and availability of domain-qualified reviewers.

Commercial terms, working hours, geography, team sizes and supported platforms are discussed case by case. These models describe engagement options, not guaranteed off-the-shelf packages.

Quality and data-handling controls

Project access, confidentiality, approved tools, reviewer permissions, handling and retention requirements are agreed for each engagement. Quality checks, exception escalation and reporting are set against the client-approved workflow and acceptance criteria. No blanket certification, regulatory or outcome guarantee is implied.

Before delivery, agree how reviewer disagreements are resolved, who approves rubric changes, when quality exceptions escalate, and what evidence is retained for acceptance review. Project-specific controls remain essential; certification does not itself guarantee that a particular AI dataset or outcome meets any accuracy threshold.
