Discovery Framework

Where should your business start with AI?

AI can support customer enquiries, employee knowledge access, document-heavy operations, and repetitive work between software systems. But not every problem calls for an AI agent or a custom model. A useful first step is understanding how work moves today and what successful improvement would look like.

We help teams ask the right questions before they commit to development:

Repetitive & Expensive Tasks

Which tasks are repetitive, slow, expensive to review, or prone to avoidable errors?

Delivery Framework

What our AI consulting process covers

A structured five-step progression from ambiguous business friction to a tested, deployable AI solution.

01Phase 1 • Problem Definition
Phase 1 • Problem Definition

Business workflow discovery

We review selected operational workflows with the people who run them. The aim is to understand the current process, recurring friction, systems involved, handoffs, volumes where available, and who owns the final decision. This establishes a concrete problem statement rather than a list of fashionable AI tools.

02Phase 2 • Value & Feasibility
Phase 2 • Value & Feasibility

AI opportunity and feasibility assessment

Potential use cases are assessed against likely business value, implementation complexity, data availability, integration constraints, risk and operational ownership. Some opportunities may be better addressed with conventional automation, search, or simpler software changes. The priority is a feasible, bounded starting point.

03Phase 3 • Technical Readiness
Phase 3 • Technical Readiness

Data, integration and security readiness

We examine what information a proposed solution would need and whether approved access is available. This may include document quality, API availability, identity and permission boundaries, human approval steps, logging, retention rules, and third-party dependencies. Sensitive use cases require additional privacy and security review before implementation.

04Phase 4 • Staged Milestones
Phase 4 • Staged Milestones

Solution architecture and implementation roadmap

After the use case is defined, we can evaluate options such as workflow automation, knowledge assistants, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), custom AI agents, or integration with existing business applications. A roadmap describes the proposed solution, assumptions, dependencies, staged milestones, responsibilities, testing gates and decisions that must be made before scale-up.

05Phase 5 • Controlled Rollout
Phase 5 • Controlled Rollout

Pilot planning and technical delivery

Frostrek AI can take a selected use case from consulting and roadmap into custom solution development, controlled pilot deployment, testing and post-launch support. Requirements, CRM or ERP interfaces where relevant, acceptance criteria, fallback paths and human oversight are defined in the project scope. Pilot results inform the decision to improve, scale or stop.

A map of the selected business workflow and its current bottlenecks.

Prioritized Use-Case Matrix

A prioritized set of AI/automation use cases with assumptions and feasibility considerations.

Data & Systems Checklist

A data and integration readiness checklist for the systems involved.

Architecture & Oversight Model

Architecture options, required controls and a human-oversight model.

Phased Implementation Plan

A phased implementation plan with dependencies, decision owners and acceptance criteria.

Pilot Measurement Framework

A measurement framework for pilot quality, effort, turnaround time or other agreed operational outcomes.

The roadmap is designed to help stakeholders make an informed implementation decision. It is not a guarantee of a particular ROI, delivery date, or model accuracy.

Practical Applications

Common AI implementation scenarios

The examples below are illustrative possibilities, not claims about completed customer projects.

Customer Enquiry & Service Workflows

Connect approved knowledge, incoming questions and human escalation paths so that teams can respond consistently and retain context. Where appropriate, compare a custom system with an existing product workflow before building from scratch.

Internal Knowledge & Document Workflows

Evaluate a searchable assistant that answers approved internal questions from policies, product documents or operating procedures, with source references and access permissions.

CRM & ERP Integration with Business Workflows

Assess opportunities to connect AI with existing CRM or ERP systems, such as enquiry triage, customer follow-ups, record updates or operational data workflows. Specific integration scope depends on the platform, available APIs, customer permissions, data quality and appropriate testing. No universal compatibility is implied.

AI Evaluation & Quality Operations

For AI product teams, define evaluation rubrics, human review checkpoints, coding or STEM subject-matter requirements, and measurable QA processes before a larger managed workstream.

Stakeholder Alignment

Designed for business and technical teams

Business leaders need to understand the likely operational value, dependencies and delivery decisions. Technical teams need clarity on data boundaries, APIs, security controls, test cases and ownership. A useful AI roadmap aligns both groups around the same scope.

Frostrek can work end-to-end, from discovery through development and deployment, or provide a roadmap for a client’s existing engineering team. Our approach is designed to help growing international businesses move from uncertainty to implementation without forcing every company toward the same AI stack.

How we measure whether a pilot is working

Success measures should match the workflow. Examples include response quality against an approved rubric, time spent on a repeatable task, escalation accuracy, rework frequency, handoff completeness, or user adoption.

A baseline and evaluation method should be agreed before results are interpreted. Outcomes depend on the use case and implementation; no universal improvement percentage is promised.
